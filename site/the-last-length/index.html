The last length

At nine twenty-six, Pauline turned at the shallow end and saw the lifeguard taking the floats out of the neighbouring lane.

She had four lengths left. She counted them in pairs because forty was less discouraging than eighty, though she knew perfectly well what she was doing. Thirty-eight. She pushed off before he could speak.

Underwater, the rope made a wavering blue line beside her shoulder. Without it, she tended to drift left. Her right arm had never quite matched her left, even before the operation, and she resented the physiotherapist's interest in making this a new thing.

At the far wall she turned again. The lifeguard had reached the steps. He was young enough to sit on his heels with both feet flat, a position she could remember using to inspect things in low cupboards. He drew the rope towards him. Each plastic float struck the next with a hollow knock.

When she reached him, she held the wall without looking up.

'Two more.'

'We close at half past.'

'I know.'

He lifted the hooked end free. The line went slack beside her.

'Can you leave that?'

He looked at the rope, then at the clock.

'For two lengths,' she said.

He hooked it back. He did not tighten it. That annoyed her more than it should have.

She pushed off hard enough to feel it in her shoulder. For the first six strokes she was swimming at him, although he was behind her. Then she settled into the pace she could sustain. Breathe towards the windows, turn towards the tiles. At this hour the windows showed only the pool again, with another lifeguard standing beyond another strip of wet floor.

At the deep end she rested a hand against the wall. The second hand on the clock juddered forward. She had time. She also had time to get out here, but her towel was at the other end.

On the return she lost the rhythm of her breathing. A little water went into her mouth. She stopped, coughing, stood up, and discovered she was already in the shallow part.

'You all right?'

She raised a hand. It could have meant yes or leave me alone. He stayed where he was.

There were perhaps eight metres left. She put her face back in and swam them, watching the black line widen into its cross.

At the steps he moved the loose rope out of her way. She took each stair with both feet. On the last one, her foot slipped half an inch, and his hand came forward without touching her.

'I'm fine.'

'Okay.'

Her towel had a cold patch where it had hung against the rail. She wrapped it round her shoulders and looked at the clock. Nine twenty-nine.

He was unhooking the rope again.

'I wasn't going to keep you,' she said.

He pulled three floats up onto the tiles before answering.

'The showers are still on.'

In the changing room someone had left a small puddle of shampoo on the bench. Pauline put her bag at the other end. She sat for a moment with her goggles in her hand, a little water caught in the rims, then shook them out onto the floor.
